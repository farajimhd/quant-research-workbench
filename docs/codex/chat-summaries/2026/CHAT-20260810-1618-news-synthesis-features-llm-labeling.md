### Narrative

The chat began inside the frozen `TASK-0182` News Synthesis audit. The user required a complete, systematic diagnosis with no case hard-coding, bounded parallel review, exact source and gold authority, and no use of development or final partitions during repair. Hundreds of 25-record mismatch chunks were reviewed under strict isolation. Workers emitted runtime-only finding and summary artifacts, while the controller tracked source coverage, material mismatch units, eligible extras, exact trace-only units, category allowlists, and hashes. The recurrent families were identity resolution, document origin and purpose, issuer role/focality, currentness, semantic eligibility, sentiment composition, source-adapter/evaluation defects, likely certified-gold defects, and genuine policy ambiguity. Later consolidation grouped thousands of findings into generic families rather than ticker- or phrase-specific patches.

The audit produced several durable engineering conclusions. Nondistinctive grammatical aliases such as generic possessive corporate references were a major false-identity source, but simply removing an alias changed entity cardinality and exposed participation logic that bound otherwise ungrounded statements to the sole surviving issuer. The correct invariant was therefore broader than alias filtering: adding or removing an entity with no local participation must not change another issuer's statement assignment, view, or eligibility. Identity-quality flags also needed provenance: source-level unresolved or ambiguous quality is document-global, whereas an emitted entity's uncertain identity should block only that entity. Flattening both into one flag list created either collateral false negatives or a fail-open when provenance was guessed from current entity statuses.

The same diagnosis found that document-wide analyst/origin/purpose fields were vetoing locally grounded issuer events. A safe long-term design requires statement-level claim origin and eligibility based on issuer-local current statements, not a single document envelope. This implies a contract migration rather than a one-line relaxation. Another source-boundary experiment showed why structural markers cannot be treated as automatic contamination: some appended external sections were junk, but others contained the substantive linked primary release or reporting source. Unconditional truncation fixed many contamination cases while introducing genuine omissions. The retained conclusion was to build provenance/content-role source selection, not marker-only deletion. Listing-state work converged on a consequence-aware rule: pure reverse splits and procedural cure windows are not automatically positive or negative; active deficiencies remain adverse, achieved compliance is favorable, and explicit cure-motivated mechanics require their stated consequence. Temporal precedence and negation had to distinguish failed-then-achieved recovery from achieved-then-lost compliance.

The user then challenged whether the deterministic system was overfitting and requested independent learned baselines. The work inventoried available Qwen embeddings, created deterministic train/validation splits, trained comparable models over Qwen, TF-IDF, and OpenAI representations, and repeatedly refined only the sparse features while holding the model fixed. The user rejected an early TF-IDF dependency on decoded Qwen token IDs because original or normalized/rendered text was the correct authority. Subsequent versions moved through normalized, original, enriched, and structured multi-view inputs. V9 became the strongest eligibility-oriented sparse representation: it combined original title/teaser/body, invariant metadata, issuer-gated enriched text, normalized structural views, issuer-local clause features, source-view agreement/conflict, semantic roles, temporal and negation state, numeric buckets, and sparse CSR storage. Its official validation result improved eligibility but not every sentiment or concept metric. V10 tested additional relational comparison and stability-selection features under five-fold development-only cross-validation; it slightly improved article eligibility and concept exact-set accuracy but weakened issuer eligibility, every sentiment class, and concept F1. The explicit outcome was not to promote V10 and to retain V9 when eligibility is primary. These completed experiments are recorded separately in `TASK-0189` and `TASK-0190`.

Attention then shifted from reproducing the existing News Synthesis schema to a shorter, independent issuer-level LLM authority. The user clarified that concepts should not be open vocabulary and should be replaced by fixed issuer-based tags; positive and negative implications should be independent probabilities; forecast eligibility needed a plain-language definition; and evidence should use compact sentence IDs rather than copied quotes. The resulting V3 schema asks the model to identify each issuer and return identity confidence, forecast-relevance probability, independent positive and negative implication probabilities, fixed event tags and issuer roles, time scope, claim source, and one to three evidence sentence IDs. Forecast relevance is high only for a material new issuer event or guidance grounded in trustworthy issuer-local evidence, but it can remain high when direction is neutral or uncertain. Analyst actions, previews, historical recaps, price observations, and references without a new underlying event remain ineligible.

The prompt was implemented in `research/text_intelligence/llm_issuer_labeling_v3`. Eleven compact examples were selected from certified gold to cover positive, negative, mixed, and neutral eligible events; analyst actions, price observations, and previews that are not forecast eligible; and a two-issuer acquisition. Example source IDs were mechanically excluded from evaluation and never sent to the model. A deterministic stratified 250-article evaluation sampled the consolidated audit source population by authority and article eligibility, filled missing publication timestamps from gold authority, persisted a separate answer key, and submitted `gpt-5.6-sol` through the OpenAI Batch API. Structured Outputs rejected `uniqueItems`, so the transport schema removed that unsupported keyword while local validation retained strict uniqueness. Four long responses were initially truncated; bounded retry lanes and, for the last two zero-progress roundup requests, identical synchronous recovery completed the exact population without changing inputs or labels.

The final run persisted 250 of 250 schema-valid label documents with zero remaining failures under `D:\TradingML\runtimes\text_intelligence\llm_issuer_labeling_v3\gold_audit_250_sol_v1`. Article forecast eligibility reached 86.40% accuracy and 92.06% F1. Issuer forecast eligibility reached 79.76% accuracy and 83.30% F1. Eligible-issuer sentiment reached 79.15% accuracy and 68.44% macro-F1. On 55 gold units with known concept-to-tag coverage, event tags reached 49.09% exact-set accuracy and 71.63% micro-F1. Exact ticker matching found 31 missing gold issuer rows, 270 additional predicted tickers, and 16 eligible additional tickers; broad roundup records contributed heavily to the extra identities, and incomplete gold identity coverage means they cannot all be assumed false. Estimated total API cost, including retries and the schema diagnostic, was about USD 4.55. The final discussion considered using fresh Codex subagents to label all 2026 news outside existing gold. The accepted design treats subagents as a controlled annotation system, not automatic human gold. A controller must first inventory the authoritative 2026 UTC population, exclude consolidated-gold source IDs, render only missing normalized articles with the canonical idempotent renderer, and freeze source/prompt/schema/example hashes. Worker agents should use `fork_turns="none"`, receive only a bounded inline packet plus the exact V3 prompt/schema/examples, use no tools or external context, and return structured labels with an isolation attestation. Packets should be token-bounded: several short items, four to six ordinary articles, and one or two long roundups. Single-pass labels are teacher/silver; a deterministic stratified and high-risk subset receives blind duplicate labeling; disagreements go to a fresh blinded adjudicator. Same-model agreement remains correlated and therefore produces Codex-agreement or Codex-adjudicated authority, not human-certified gold. ### Durable decisions

- Preserve deterministic News Synthesis, sparse learned baselines, and standalone LLM/Codex labeling as separate authorities.
- Do not tune on sealed development/final populations or inject gold tickers, labels, News Synthesis predictions, later prices, or external context into labeler packets.
- Use original authoritative source identity and hashes; normalized text is a derived, versioned input produced by the canonical renderer.
- Forecast relevance is issuer-local material-event eligibility and is independent of positive/negative direction.
- Keep positive and negative implication probabilities independent; both high means mixed and both low means neutral/uncertain.
- Use fixed event tags and roles, compact evidence sentence IDs, and strict structured validation.
- Preserve V9 as the preferred sparse eligibility representation; V10 is negative experimental evidence and must not be promoted.
- Do not call one-pass or same-model-agreement labels human-certified gold. Record explicit `codex_single_pass`, `codex_agreement_confirmed`, `codex_adjudicated`, and future `human_certified` levels.
- For future workers, use no inherited chat context, no tools, bounded inline packets, runtime-only outputs, and explicit isolation attestations.
- Do not truncate oversized articles or silently substitute another source/renderer; isolate them into smaller packets with sufficient output headroom.
