### Narrative

The chat continued a long News Synthesis and forecast-eligibility program. The user first asked for a review of a handoff prompt and its subagent strategy, emphasizing minimum token use, fastest execution, high accuracy, bounded worker context, and prompt cleanup after completion. The substantive issue was then clarified: Benzinga news already had 2025 through August 2026 forecast-eligibility supervision, a TF-IDF-plus-metadata labeler, and deterministic News Synthesis, but the desired top-of-funnel filter was supposed to eliminate obvious provider-generated noise before expensive analysis. The user believed Benzinga's algorithmic tags, channels, templates, timing, ticker multiplicity, and related metadata could expose deterministic paths. The work therefore shifted away from generic text-only classification toward an evidence-led analysis of provider metadata and causal article context.

The user defined three intended funnel functions: reject noise; synthesize important issuer news and possibly sentiment from language; and later synthesize noise as market, analyst, and trading-idea context. The immediate scope remained forecast eligibility. A feature-analysis pipeline measured tags, channels, tag/channel combinations, template signatures, title and text flags, publication session, ticker count, first-news status, session ordinal, elapsed time since prior ticker news, rendered length, and later causal market-cap context. It produced 709 forward-screened provider paths. A separate reviewer labeled all 709 paths `likely_eligible` or `likely_ineligible` without seeing supports, label rates, odds ratios, mutual information, temporal grades, waterfall results, or prior reports. Joining those blind semantic opinions to hidden empirical labels exposed 2,767 currently eligible articles under paths judged likely ineligible.

All 2,767 contradictions received prediction-blind full-text review. Reviewers saw opaque IDs and exact rendered text, not current labels, matched paths, tags, channels, statistical results, or prior votes. Every proposed change, insufficient decision, or low-confidence case received a second read; disagreements received a third. The audit retained 2,130 eligible labels, corrected 636 to ineligible and one to insufficient, and left one three-distinct-label case unchanged. It established that `bzi-ep` strongly represented scheduled earnings previews, while the apparently obvious `bzi-recaps` interpretation was wrong: all 2,009 reviewed contradictions were current eligible issuer events. Mover and market-update families were mixed and required semantic rescue. This was an important rejection of literal provider-code interpretation and automatic path-based flipping.

The 709 paths did not cover 160,360 decisive articles, so a residual analysis examined 80,020 observed representations and nominated 423 prevalent paths. It found no additional forward-stable path with an eligible rate at or below five percent in every period; the obvious global metadata-only noise rules were largely exhausted. The two catalogs were merged into a fixed 1,132-path authority. Later correction-grade campaigns reviewed 6,896 trading-ideas candidates, changing 1,609 eligible labels to ineligible and four to insufficient, then reviewed all 960 previously unreviewed stable-path exceptions. That exception audit changed 129 eligible labels to ineligible and 53 ineligible labels to eligible. A refreshed path created eight new `long ideas|markets` exceptions; two independent full-text readers changed seven and retained one. The final path authority has 104 stable-ineligible paths, 15 stable-eligible paths, and 2,102 current exceptions, all covered by a review or certification source. No path exception remains unreviewed.

The reviewed path findings were incorporated into provider-context router and funnel V4, followed by causal market-cap-aware V5 refinements. High-support zero-exception analyst channel pairs became hard context filters. `long ideas|markets` became mandatory semantic rescue because 35 reviewed eligible counterexamples remained. The exact two-ticker, no-tag, M&A-plus-news signature became an event prior rather than a forcing rule because six reviewed ineligible counterexamples remained. Market cap was used only in combination with context and only when point-in-time evidence was causally available. On 346,103 corrected development articles, Funnel V4 routed 130,710 to context-only processing, retained every eligible article, and recorded zero eligible false rejections. Its frozen 1,000-article holdout routed 288 articles through fast context with zero eligible false rejection, while overall deterministic synthesis accuracy remained 79.20% and balanced accuracy 73.53%. The safe gate solved compute rejection, not the downstream semantic limitations of full synthesis.

The user then separated deterministic synthesis from a learned structured classifier. A frozen 11,434-dimensional structured feature contract encoded provider categories, every tag and channel from the 2010-2025 historical dictionary, source-quality flags, lexical event flags, cyclic time, session segment, ticker count and article length, ticker-session novelty and recency, and causal market-cap fields. Unknown post-training categories had explicit buckets. Random Forests were trained in both temporal directions against progressively corrected supervision. A 1,000-row prediction-blind disagreement calibration changed 741 labels, confirmed 229, and preserved 30 unresolved. The 12,099-row current-ineligible/model-eligible priority campaign changed 11,868 to eligible, confirmed 223 ineligible, and preserved eight unresolved. Its compact easy lane reached 99.47% agreement with a 98.85% Wilson lower bound under mandatory and stratified full-text QA. The reverse 16,680-row disagreement campaign changed 4,237 labels, retained 12,400, and preserved 43 unresolved.

These correction-grade audits produced the finalized development authority `forecast_eligibility_sentiment_authority_structured_rf_reverse_audit_v1`: 361,695 unique articles, comprising 151,632 eligible, 208,650 ineligible, and 1,413 insufficient-short-text labels. Gold issuer sentiment remained byte-identical throughout. This large authority is not equivalent to human gold: most 2025-2026 rows are Codex-labeled or multi-reader-adjudicated supervision with explicit provenance. The independent consolidated certified gold remains 18,144 articles and 28,037 issuer units; gold-only sentiment contains 16,983 issuer units. The model-directed 2025 and 2026 populations ceased to be untouched test evidence after their disagreement audits.

Retraining the structured Random Forest on 2025 and evaluating 2026 gave 88.060% accuracy, 88.345% balanced accuracy, 86.594% eligible F1, 95.014% ROC AUC, and 16,985 disagreements. Training on 2026 and evaluating 2025 gave 93.896% accuracy, 94.376% balanced accuracy, 92.406% eligible F1, 98.612% ROC AUC, and 12,443 disagreements. These are finalized-label agreement diagnostics, not honest forward accuracy, because the evaluation labels had been selected for model-disagreement correction.

To restore independent evidence, the work froze all 5,044 Benzinga articles after the previously exposed August 13, 2026 boundary. Review packets hid model predictions, probabilities, prior supervision, split membership, and source IDs. Two compact readers handled every article, but the precommitted compact fast-lane QA reached only 89.01% agreement with full text, below the 99% agreement and 98% Wilson-lower-bound requirements. The fast lane was rejected and all 5,044 articles received full-text review. Independent second and third readers resolved 4,913 labels—1,827 eligible and 3,086 ineligible—while 131 ambiguous records remained explicitly unresolved.

The two frozen single-year structured forests achieved roughly 82.6-83.0% accuracy on this genuinely time-forward holdout. A combined pre-boundary structured forest trained on all 346,103 decisive articles through August 13 achieved 84.14% accuracy at its internally selected 0.44 threshold and 85.24% at fixed 0.50. Training-set resubstitution had only 210 mismatches at 0.44 and three at 0.50, confirming that near-perfect training agreement did not imply generalization. Adding a training-only 75,000-dimensional word unigram/bigram TF-IDF vocabulary created 86,434 total features. The structured-plus-TF-IDF forest reached 84.88% accuracy at selected threshold 0.48. A sparse MLP reached 83.84% accuracy and 91.24% eligible recall at selected threshold 0.45.

The final challenger was sparse DeepFM over the identical structured-plus-TF-IDF matrix: a wide linear branch, 32-dimensional feature embeddings, explicit second-order factorization interactions, and a 128-unit residual deep branch. Its validation-selected threshold 0.38 produced 83.33% holdout accuracy, 85.06% balanced accuracy, 91.79% eligible recall, 80.37% eligible F1, and 93.88% ROC AUC. Fixed 0.50 produced the stronger observed result: 85.24% accuracy, 86.17% balanced accuracy, 89.76% eligible recall, 81.90% eligible F1, and the same 93.88% ROC AUC. DeepFM was proposed only after RF and MLP holdout results had been inspected, so this is iterative benchmark evidence, not pristine architecture-selection evidence; no further tuning on this holdout is permitted.

The deterministic V5 `context_only` gate was then applied independently to the August holdout. It rejected 1,542 articles, or 30.57% of all 5,044 and 31.39% of the 4,913 resolved population. All 1,542 were gold ineligible, capturing 49.97% of true ineligibles with zero eligible false rejection. Offline intersection showed DeepFM predicted none of those 1,542 eligible at either 0.38 or 0.50. This established strong historical compatibility between the safe deterministic lane and DeepFM, but the user made a later operational decision: future data should use DeepFM-only because lexical TF-IDF evidence may adapt better to provider drift; deterministic rejection remains acceptable for historical labeling and analysis.

Finally, the historical hybrid—deterministic final-ineligible gate followed by DeepFM only on the remainder—was run over every 2024 article in current ClickHouse FINAL event and rendered authorities. All 218,040 unique articles had rendered text. The gate rejected 87,199 (39.99%), and DeepFM scored 130,841 (60.01%) with zero empty TF-IDF vectors. At selected threshold 0.38, final predictions were 85,622 eligible (39.27%) and 132,418 ineligible (60.73%); the latter consisted of 87,199 gate rejections plus 45,219 DeepFM rejections. At fixed 0.50, predictions were 83,351 eligible and 134,689 ineligible. Causal market-cap evidence was unavailable for 2024, so no market-cap rule fired and market-cap model fields remained explicitly missing. The prediction ledger, report, and hashes validated all IDs, stages, probabilities, counts, and SHA-256 values. Because 2024 has no reviewed forecast labels, these are prediction distributions, not accuracy results.
